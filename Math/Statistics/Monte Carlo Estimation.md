**Monte Carlo estimation** approximates an [expected value](Expected%20Value.md) by averaging random samples, which is how reinforcement learning estimates returns and gradients without knowing the environment's probabilities.

---
## Definition
$$
\boxed{\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p}[f(x)] \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} f(x_i), \qquad x_i \sim p}
$$ where:
- **$p$:** Distribution being sampled; only samples are needed, not its formula.
- **$f$:** Function whose average is wanted.
- **$x_i$:** The $i$-th independent sample.
- **$N$:** Number of samples.

The estimate is unbiased, and its standard error shrinks as $\sigma / \sqrt{N}$, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of $f(x)$.
## Example
Estimate the expected payout of a lottery that pays 10 with probability $0.2$ and 0 otherwise (true value 2).
### Step 1: Draw samples
Five draws give $x = 10, 0, 0, 0, 0$.
### Step 2: Average
$$
\frac{10 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0}{5} = 2
$$
With other draws the estimate would vary (e.g. two wins give $20/5 = 4$); more samples make it more reliable.
## Discussion
In reinforcement learning, **Monte Carlo** methods estimate a [value function](../../Machine%20Learning/Reinforcement%20Learning/Value%20Function.md) by averaging complete observed returns, and REINFORCE estimates the [policy gradient](../../Machine%20Learning/Reinforcement%20Learning/Policy%20Gradient.md) the same way. [Temporal difference learning](../../Machine%20Learning/Reinforcement%20Learning/Temporal%20Difference%20Learning.md) is the lower-variance alternative that bootstraps instead of waiting for full returns.
