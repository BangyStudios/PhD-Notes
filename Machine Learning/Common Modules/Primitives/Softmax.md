The **softmax** function turns a vector of real-valued scores (**logits**) into a probability distribution, and it is the standard output layer for classification and for discrete stochastic policies.

---
## Definition
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} \exp(z_j / T)}}
$$ where:
- **$\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^K$:** Vector of logits.
- **$z_i$:** Logit of class (or action) $i$.
- **$K$:** Number of classes.
- **$T > 0$:** **Temperature**; $T = 1$ is the standard softmax.

The outputs are positive and sum to 1. The gradient of the log-probability, used in cross-entropy training and [policy gradients](../../Reinforcement%20Learning/Policy%20Gradient.md), is:
$$
\frac{\partial \log \operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i}{\partial z_j} = \mathbb{1}[i = j] - \operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z})_j
$$ where:
- **$\mathbb{1}[i = j]$:** 1 if $i = j$ and 0 otherwise ($T = 1$).
## Example
Logits $\mathbf{z} = [2, 1, 0]$ with $T = 1$.
### Step 1: Exponentiate
$$
[e^2, e^1, e^0] = [7.389,\, 2.718,\, 1.000]
$$
### Step 2: Normalize
The sum is $7.389 + 2.718 + 1.000 = 11.107$:
$$
\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z}) = \left[\frac{7.389}{11.107},\, \frac{2.718}{11.107},\, \frac{1.000}{11.107}\right] \approx [0.665,\, 0.245,\, 0.090]
$$
## Discussion
- **Numerical stability:** subtracting $\max_j z_j$ from every logit leaves the result unchanged and prevents overflow in $\exp$.
- **Temperature:** as $T \to 0$ the output approaches a one-hot argmax; as $T \to \infty$ it approaches the uniform distribution.
