Cross attention is an attention mechanism where the **queries come from one sequence and the keys and values come from a separate sequence**, enabling every element of the first sequence to selectively retrieve information from the second; in the standard encoder-decoder Transformer this means each decoder token can attend over the full encoder output, but the mechanism is general to any two sequences.

---
## Definition
In [2.1-Self Attention](2.1-Self%20Attention.md) the three projections Q, K, V are all derived from the *same* input. Cross attention relaxes this: given two sequences $X_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ (the **query sequence**) and $X_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$ (the **key-value sequence**), the projections are split across sources:
$$
Q = X_1 W_Q, \qquad K = X_2 W_K, \qquad V = X_2 W_V
$$
where:
- **$X_1$:** The sequence whose elements ask questions — each row becomes one query vector.
- **$X_2$:** The sequence being read from — its rows supply both the keys (what to match against) and the values (what to return).
- **$W_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_k}$, $W_K \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_k}$, $W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_v}$:** Learned projection matrices.

The output is then computed with the standard scaled dot-product formula:
$$
\text{CrossAttention}(X_1, X_2) = \text{softmax}_\text{row}\!\left(\frac{(X_1 W_Q)(X_2 W_K)^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)(X_2 W_V)
$$

The attention weight matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ has entry $A_{ij}$ measuring how strongly query $i$ (from $X_1$) should attend to key $j$ (from $X_2$). Each output row is therefore a weighted sum of the **value vectors of $X_2$**, with weights determined by how well the corresponding query aligns with each key. Because Q and K,V originate from different sequences, there is no constraint that $n = m$ — $X_1$ and $X_2$ can have different lengths.

In the encoder-decoder Transformer, $X_1$ is the decoder's current hidden state and $X_2$ is the encoder output, so each target token can attend over all source tokens.

## Example
### Setup
Suppose we have an encoder-decoder setup with $d=2$:
- **Query sequence** $X_1$ (decoder state, 2 target tokens):
$$
X_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
- **Key-value sequence** $X_2 = E$ (encoder output, 3 source tokens: "The", "cat", "sat"):
$$
X_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$
- Use identity projections: $W_Q = W_K = W_V = I$, so $Q = X_1$, $K = V = X_2$.

---
### Step 1: Compute raw attention scores

$$
QK^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Each entry $(i,j)$ is the dot-product similarity between query token $i$ (from $X_1$) and key token $j$ (from $X_2$), measuring how relevant source token $j$ is to target token $i$.

---
### Step 2: Scale by $\sqrt{d_k}$

$$
\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.71 & 0.00 & 0.71 \\ 0.00 & 0.71 & 0.71 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---
### Step 3: Apply softmax row-wise

- Row 1: $[0.71, 0.00, 0.71]$ → softmax ≈ $[0.40, 0.20, 0.40]$
- Row 2: $[0.00, 0.71, 0.71]$ → softmax ≈ $[0.20, 0.40, 0.40]$

$$
A \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.40 & 0.20 & 0.40 \\ 0.20 & 0.40 & 0.40 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---
### Step 4: Multiply by V

$$
\text{CrossAttention}(X_1, E) = A \cdot V =
\begin{bmatrix} 0.40 & 0.20 & 0.40 \\ 0.20 & 0.40 & 0.40 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} 0.80 & 0.60 \\ 0.60 & 0.80 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---
### Interpretation
- Target token 1 (row 1) focuses equally on source tokens 0 and 2 (weight 0.40 each) and less on source token 1 (weight 0.20).
- Target token 2 (row 2) focuses equally on source tokens 1 and 2 (weight 0.40 each) and less on source token 0 (weight 0.20).
- Each query token produces a weighted combination of the **value vectors of $X_2$**, with weights determined by how well its query vector aligns with each key. This is the core mechanism: cross attention lets every element of one sequence selectively read from another.
