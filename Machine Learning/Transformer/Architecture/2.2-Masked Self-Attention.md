In a transformer, every token can _potentially_ attend to every other token.  
But in many tasks, this “fully open attention” is **not what we want**.

**Attention masks** are boolean (or 0/−∞) matrices that tell the attention mechanism:

> **Which token pairs are allowed to interact, and which must be ignored.**

The mask is added to the attention logits _before_ the $\text{softmax}$.  
A masked position is given a value like **−1e9**, which $\text{softmax}$ turns into **zero attention weight**.

There are two common types of masks:

1. **Padding masks** — prevent attention to padding tokens.
2. **Causal masks** — prevent attention to future tokens.

---
## How the Mask Is Applied
Consider the standard [2.1-Self Attention](2.1-Self%20Attention.md) formula:
$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}_\text{row}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
$$
We can apply a mask by adding it to the logits before the $\text{softmax}$ operation:
$$
\text{MaskedAttention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}_\text{row}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right)V
$$
where
$$
M_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if attention is allowed} \\
-\infty & \text{if attention is blocked}
\end{cases}
$$
(In practice, $-\infty$ is approximated by a very large negative number such as $-10^9$.)
Since $\text{softmax}(-\infty)\approx 0$, masked positions receive zero attention weight.

---
## Padding Masks
When processing a batch of sequences, shorter sequences are padded to match the length of the longest sequence.

Example:

| Original | Padded |
|----------|---------|
| The cat sat | The cat sat |
| Dogs bark | Dogs bark PAD |

The PAD token contains no useful information, so other tokens should not attend to it.

For the sequence
$$
[\text{Dogs},\ \text{bark},\ \text{PAD}]
$$
the padding mask is:
$$
M_{\text{pad}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & -\infty \\
0 & 0 & -\infty \\
0 & 0 & -\infty
\end{bmatrix}
$$
This blocks attention to the PAD token (the third column).

Padding masks are commonly used in:

- Encoder-only models (e.g. BERT)
- Encoder-decoder models (e.g. translation transformers)
- Decoder-only models when batching sequences of different lengths

---
## Causal Masks
A **causal mask** prevents a token from seeing future tokens.

This is required for autoregressive language models, where token $i$ should only use information from positions $\le i$.

For the sequence
$$
[\text{The},\ \text{cat},\ \text{sat}]
$$
the causal mask is:
$$
M_{\text{causal}} =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & -\infty & -\infty \\
0 & 0 & -\infty \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$
This lower-triangular structure ensures that each token can attend only to itself and earlier tokens.

---
### Example
Consider (from [2.1-Self Attention](2.1-Self%20Attention.md)):
$$
\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.71 & 0.00 & 0.71 \\ 0.00 & 0.71 & 0.71 \\ 0.71 & 0.71 & 1.41 \end{bmatrix}
$$
Suppose we apply the following **causal mask**:
$$ 
M_{\text{causal}} = \begin{bmatrix}  
0 & -\infty & -\infty \\  
0 & 0 & -\infty \\  
0 & 0 & 0  
\end{bmatrix}
$$
We add the mask to the scaled logits:
$$
\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{2}} + M_{\text{causal}}
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}  
0.71 & -\infty & -\infty \\  
0.00 & 0.71 & -\infty \\  
0.71 & 0.71 & 1.41  
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Apply softmax row-wise ($\text{softmax}(-\infty) = 0$):

- Row 1: $[0.71, -\infty, -\infty]$ → $[1.00, 0.00, 0.00]$ ("The" attends only to itself)
- Row 2: $[0.00, 0.71, -\infty]$ → $[0.33, 0.67, 0.00]$ ("cat" attends to "The" and itself)
- Row 3: $[0.71, 0.71, 1.41]$ → $[0.25, 0.25, 0.50]$ ("sat" attends to all, same as unmasked)

$$
A_{\text{masked}} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 1.00 & 0.00 & 0.00 \\ 0.33 & 0.67 & 0.00 \\ 0.25 & 0.25 & 0.50 \end{bmatrix}
$$
Multiply by $V$:
$$
Z_{\text{masked}} = A_{\text{masked}} \cdot V =
\begin{bmatrix} 1.00 & 0.00 & 0.00 \\ 0.33 & 0.67 & 0.00 \\ 0.25 & 0.25 & 0.50 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} 1.00 & 2.00 \\ 2.34 & 3.34 \\ 3.50 & 4.50 \end{bmatrix}
$$

---
## Combining Masks
In practice, transformers often combine both mask types:
$$
M = M_{\text{causal}} + M_{\text{pad}}
$$
This ensures that:

1. Tokens cannot attend to future positions.
2. Tokens cannot attend to padding tokens.

Decoder-only models such as GPT use causal masks and often padding masks during batched training, while encoder models such as BERT typically use only padding masks.
