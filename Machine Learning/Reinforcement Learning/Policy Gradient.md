**Policy gradient** methods learn a parameterized [policy](Policy.md) directly by gradient ascent on its expected return, which works naturally for stochastic and continuous-action policies.

---
## Definition
The objective is the expected [return](Discounted%20Return.md) of the policy $\pi_\theta$:
$$
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}\!\left[ G_0 \right]
$$ where:
- **$\theta$:** Policy parameters.
- **$\tau = (s_0, a_0, r_1, s_1, \dots)$:** A trajectory generated by running $\pi_\theta$.
- **$G_0$:** Discounted return of the trajectory.

The **policy gradient theorem** gives its gradient without differentiating through the environment:
$$
\boxed{\nabla_\theta J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[ \sum_{t} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\, G_t \right]}
$$ where:
- **$\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)$:** Direction in parameter space that makes $a_t$ more likely.
- **$G_t$:** Return from time $t$, which scales how much $a_t$ is reinforced.

It follows from the **log-derivative trick**, $\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta = \pi_\theta\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta$. **REINFORCE** estimates the expectation with sampled episodes (see [Monte Carlo Estimation](../../Math/Statistics/Monte%20Carlo%20Estimation.md)) and updates $\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha \sum_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\, G_t$.
## Example
One state, two actions, and a [softmax](../Common%20Modules/Primitives/Softmax.md) policy with logits $\theta = [\theta_1, \theta_2] = [0, 0]$, so $\pi = [0.5, 0.5]$. The agent samples $a_1$ and the episode ends with return $G_0 = 1$. Learning rate $\alpha = 0.1$.
### Step 1: Gradient of the log-probability
For a softmax, $\partial \log \pi(a) / \partial \theta_b = \mathbb{1}[a = b] - \pi(b)$:
$$
\nabla_\theta \log \pi(a_1) = [1 - 0.5,\; 0 - 0.5] = [0.5,\, -0.5]
$$
### Step 2: REINFORCE update
$$
\theta \leftarrow [0, 0] + 0.1 \times 1 \times [0.5,\, -0.5] = [0.05,\, -0.05]
$$
### Step 3: New policy
$$
\pi(a_1) = \frac{e^{0.05}}{e^{0.05} + e^{-0.05}} = \frac{1.0513}{1.0513 + 0.9512} \approx 0.525
$$
The rewarded action became more likely, from 0.5 to 0.525.
## Discussion
- REINFORCE gradients have high variance. Subtracting a **baseline** $b(s_t)$ that does not depend on the action keeps the gradient unbiased and reduces variance: $\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}\big[\sum_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\,(G_t - b(s_t))\big]$.
- Choosing $b(s) = V(s)$ turns $G_t - b(s_t)$ into an estimate of the [advantage](Advantage%20Function.md), which leads to [Actor-Critic](Actor-Critic.md) and [PPO](PPO.md).
- Policy gradient is on-policy: each update needs fresh samples from the current $\pi_\theta$.
