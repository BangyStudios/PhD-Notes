**Proximal policy optimization (PPO)** is an [actor-critic](Actor-Critic.md) method that reuses each batch of experience for several gradient steps while clipping the objective so the new policy stays close to the one that collected the data.

---
## Definition
PPO maximizes the **clipped surrogate objective**:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t\!\left[ \min\!\left( \rho_t(\theta)\, \hat{A}_t,\; \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\rho_t(\theta),\, 1 - \varepsilon,\, 1 + \varepsilon\right) \hat{A}_t \right) \right]}, \qquad \rho_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
$$ where:
- **$\pi_\theta$:** Current [policy](Policy.md) being optimized.
- **$\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$:** Policy that collected the batch, held fixed during the update.
- **$\rho_t(\theta)$:** **Probability ratio**; $\rho_t = 1$ when the two policies agree on $a_t$.
- **$\hat{A}_t$:** Estimated [advantage](Advantage%20Function.md), usually from GAE.
- **$\varepsilon$:** Clip range, typically $0.2$.
- **$\operatorname{clip}(x, l, u)$:** Clamps $x$ to the interval $[l, u]$.

The $\min$ takes the more pessimistic of the two terms, so once the ratio moves outside $[1 - \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon]$ in the direction the advantage favors, the objective goes flat and its gradient is zero.
## Example
Take $\varepsilon = 0.2$, so the clip range is $[0.8, 1.2]$.
### Step 1: Good action, small change
$\hat{A} = 2$, $\rho = 1.1$:
$$
\min(1.1 \times 2,\; 1.1 \times 2) = 2.2
$$
The ratio is inside the range, so the gradient keeps pushing $\rho$ up.
### Step 2: Good action, large change
$\hat{A} = 2$, $\rho = 1.5$:
$$
\min(1.5 \times 2,\; 1.2 \times 2) = \min(3.0,\, 2.4) = 2.4
$$
The clipped term wins and is constant in $\theta$, so there is no incentive to raise $\rho$ further.
### Step 3: Bad action, large change
$\hat{A} = -1$, $\rho = 0.5$:
$$
\min(0.5 \times (-1),\; 0.8 \times (-1)) = \min(-0.5,\, -0.8) = -0.8
$$
Again the clipped term wins, so there is no incentive to lower $\rho$ below $0.8$.
## Discussion
- The full loss adds a critic regression term and an entropy bonus that encourages exploration: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}} - c_1 (V_w(s_t) - \hat{G}_t)^2 + c_2\, H[\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s_t)]$, where $\hat{G}_t$ is the return target and $c_1, c_2$ are weights.
- PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) simplifies **TRPO**, which enforces a hard limit on the [KL divergence](../Miscellaneous/KL%20Divergence.md) between old and new policies.
- PPO is the standard optimizer in reinforcement learning from human feedback (**RLHF**) for language models.
