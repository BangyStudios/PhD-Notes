The **Bellman equation** writes the value of a state as the immediate reward plus the discounted value of the next state, and this recursion is the basis of almost every reinforcement learning algorithm.

---
## Definition
Substituting $G_t = r_{t+1} + \gamma G_{t+1}$ into the [value function](Value%20Function.md) gives the **Bellman expectation equation** for a policy $\pi$:
$$
V^\pi(s) = \sum_{a} \pi(a \mid s) \left[ R(s, a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s' \mid s, a)\, V^\pi(s') \right]
$$ where:
- **$\pi(a \mid s)$:** Probability that the [policy](Policy.md) picks $a$ in $s$.
- **$R(s, a)$:** Expected immediate reward.
- **$P(s' \mid s, a)$:** Transition probability to next state $s'$.
- **$\gamma$:** Discount factor.

For the optimal action-value function, the average over actions becomes a max, giving the **Bellman optimality equation**:
$$
\boxed{Q^*(s, a) = R(s, a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s' \mid s, a)\, \max_{a'} Q^*(s', a')}
$$ where:
- **$Q^*(s, a)$:** Optimal action value.
- **$a'$:** Action taken in the next state.
## Example
Check the optimal Q-values of the two-state MDP from [Markov Decision Process](Markov%20Decision%20Process.md) with $\gamma = 0.9$. Transitions are deterministic, so the sum over $s'$ has one term. The claimed values are $Q^*(s_2, \text{stay}) = 10$, $Q^*(s_1, \text{move}) = 9$, and $Q^*(s_1, \text{stay}) = Q^*(s_2, \text{move}) = 8.1$.
### Step 1: Stay in $s_2$
$$
Q^*(s_2, \text{stay}) = 1 + 0.9 \times \max(10,\, 8.1) = 1 + 9 = 10 \;\checkmark
$$
### Step 2: Move from $s_1$
$$
Q^*(s_1, \text{move}) = 0 + 0.9 \times \max(10,\, 8.1) = 9 \;\checkmark
$$
### Step 3: The two remaining actions
Both lead to $s_1$ with reward 0:
$$
Q^*(s_1, \text{stay}) = Q^*(s_2, \text{move}) = 0 + 0.9 \times \max(8.1,\, 9) = 8.1 \;\checkmark
$$
The greedy policy is $\text{move}$ in $s_1$ and $\text{stay}$ in $s_2$.
## Discussion
- **Value iteration** applies the optimality equation repeatedly as an update, $Q \leftarrow R + \gamma P \max Q$; it converges to $Q^*$ because each update shrinks errors by a factor of $\gamma$.
- When $P$ and $R$ are unknown, [Q-Learning](Q-Learning.md) replaces the sum over $s'$ with a single sampled transition.
