**Experience replay** stores past transitions in a buffer and trains on random mini-batches drawn from it, which breaks the correlation between consecutive samples and lets each transition be reused many times.

---
## Definition
The agent appends each transition to a first-in, first-out buffer of fixed capacity and samples training batches uniformly:
$$
\mathcal{D} = \{(s_i, a_i, r_i, s'_i)\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{D}|}, \qquad P(i) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}
$$ where:
- **$\mathcal{D}$:** Replay buffer, holding at most $N$ transitions; the oldest is evicted when it is full.
- **$(s_i, a_i, r_i, s'_i)$:** One stored transition.
- **$P(i)$:** Probability of drawing transition $i$ in one draw.
## Example
### Step 1: Insert into a full buffer
Capacity $N = 3$, buffer $\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$. Adding $e_4$ evicts the oldest:
$$
\{e_1, e_2, e_3\} \;\to\; \{e_2, e_3, e_4\}
$$
### Step 2: Sample a batch
Drawing one transition uniformly gives each a probability of $P(i) = 1/3$. Drawing a batch of 2 without replacement includes a given transition with probability $1 - \tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{2}{3}$.
## Discussion
- Replayed data comes from older policies, so only off-policy learners such as [Q-Learning](Q-Learning.md) and [DQN](DQN.md) can use it directly.
- **Prioritized experience replay** samples transitions with large TD error more often, $P(i) \propto |\delta_i|^{\alpha}$, where $\alpha$ controls how strong the prioritization is.
